Most of the software we use every day is rented. The documents, the notes, the memory our AI tools are starting to keep: they all live somewhere we don't control, in formats we can't read, behind terms that can change on a Tuesday.

I don't think that's inevitable. I think it's mostly a convenience gap. Open source alternatives often exist, but they ask for a weekend of setup, a tolerance for rough edges, and a background most people don't have.

Open source shouldn't only be for people who already know how it works.

What b32n is

b32n is a small studio for closing that gap. It builds open tools that are pleasant to use, quick to start with, and designed so your data stays yours. Today that's two projects:

  • Spindle, forever-free spreadsheet, document and slide editors for React, so you can build an office suite without renting one.
  • Mindmaps, a memory you and your agents share, made of cards you can actually open and read.

Why the little dots

You'll notice the site is full of tiny particles that keep coming together into shapes. That's the idea in miniature: lots of small, simple, open pieces assembling into something useful. No single piece is impressive. Together they make something you can own.

What to expect here

I'll write about the projects as they grow, the decisions behind them, and the broader question of how to make open software approachable. Some posts will be technical. Some won't. All of them will be honest about what works and what doesn't yet.

If any of this resonates, the code is on GitHub, and the RSS feed is the easiest way to follow along.

Thanks for stopping by.
